A Pre-Homo sapiens Vowel Inventory Preserved in the Śivasūtras: Evidence from Comparative Primatology and Hominin Vocal Tract Evolution

Keyur Joshi Independent Researcher, Amdavad, Gujarat, India
Corresponding Author: keyur1277@gmail.com

Abstract

This paper estimates the terminus post quem and terminus ante quem for the phonemic inventory (varṇamālā) preserved in the Śivasūtras using paleoanthropological evidence of vocal tract evolution[7]. The provisional terminus post quem (~7 Ma) is established by the phylogenetic emergence of consonantal production capacity at the Homo–Pan divergence[8]. The terminus ante quem (~430 ka) is constrained by the loss of the anatomical ability to produce the vowels ऋ and ऌ as described in the Pāṇinīya Śikṣā and Ṛgveda–Prātiśākhya[9]. These vowels require tongue root articulation at the hard palate and teeth, respectively, which became anatomically impossible following laryngeal descent in Homo sapiens[10]. The phonemic system preserved in the Śivasūtras thus reflects vocal tract constraints of pre-sapiens hominins, likely Homo erectus or related species[11]. This hypothesis generates falsifiable predictions through acoustic modeling of reconstructed Homo erectus sensu lato vocal tracts[12].

Scope & Methodology

The Śivasūtras comprise two distinct phonetic sections: vowels (svaras) and consonants (vyañjanas)[14]. Chronological bounds for the Śivasūtras are established by evaluating the phylogenetic emergence of anatomical capacities for vowels and the Homo–Pan divergence for consonants[16, 17]. The terminus ante quem is inferred from comparison with articulatory descriptions in the Pāṇinīya Śikṣā and related texts against paleoanthropological constraints on relevant anatomy[19, 22]. This analysis treats these texts as a sophisticated phonetic-phonological systematization preserving fundamental articulatory categories of an inherited speech system[29].

Phonetic Framework & Evolutionary Inference

Vowels

The vowel inventory in the Śivasūtras comprises three categories: quantal (अ, इ, उ, ए, ओ), derived (ऐ, औ), and unique (ऋ, ऌ)[33].

  • Quantal Vowels: These exhibit stable formant patterns that minimize acoustic-perceptual confusion[36]. Recent studies on Guinea baboons (Papio papio) establish that vowel-like segments (VLSs) matching human [ɑ, ɨ, u, æ, ɔ] are possible without laryngeal descent[39, 41]. These findings establish a terminus post quem for the quantal vowel substrate at approximately 25 Ma[46].
  • Derived Vowels: The diphthongs ऐ ([ai]) and औ ([au]) require combinatorial sequencing[49]. Such sequencing capacities have been documented in non-human primates, including Campbell’s monkeys and chimpanzees[51, 52].
  • Unique Vowels (ऋ, ऌ): The Pāṇinīya Śikṣā and Ṛgveda–Prātiśākhya describe ऋ and ऌ as non-contact (aspṛśya) sounds produced by the tongue root (jihvāmūla) at the hard palate (mūrdha) and teeth (dantya)[63].

In modern Homo sapiens, the tongue root is mechanically unconfigured to approximate the hard palate as described[74]. However, this geometry was anatomically feasible in the ancestral high-larynx configuration found in Australopithecus afarensis (~3.3 Ma) and likely retained in Homo erectus[82, 85].

Synthesis: Phylogenetic Stratification

  • Layer 1 (~25 Ma): Deep-time acoustic substrate (quantal vowels)[144, 145].
  • Layer 2 (7–5 Ma): Hominin-specific consonantal capacities following Homo–Pan divergence[146, 147].
  • Layer 3 (~430 ka or earlier): Pre-sapiens phonemic elaboration (ऋ, ऌ, absence of आ, dual ह) optimized for Homo erectus anatomy[149].
  • Layer 4 (430 ka–present): Cultural transmission and formalization in the Vaidic tradition[150, 151].
