# लेट् and the Roots of Reference

### Folio entry — brainstorm log

---

## 1. Starting point: two timelines

**Object permanence** (ability to mentally represent a real, concrete object that is not currently perceptible):

- Ontogeny: rudimentary at ~8 months (error-prone), reliable for visible displacements by ~12–18 months, full (invisible-displacement) permanence by ~18–24 months.
- Phylogeny: not a single inherited trait. Convergently evolved — independently in mammals and in birds (corvids, some parrots), lineages that split ~300+ million years ago. Within primates specifically, full (stage-6) permanence is reliably shown across the whole great-ape clade (chimps, bonobos, gorillas, orangutans) and only inconsistently in monkeys and prosimians — best treated as a great-ape-clade-level capacity in this lineage, likely present by the last common ancestor of great apes (~13–16 mya) or at least of Pan/Homo (~6–7 mya), rather than traceable to a much deeper ancestor.
- Method note: this is inference from the distribution of the trait across living relatives (comparative method + parsimony), not fossil-dated observation — which is as good as it gets for a purely cognitive trait. Brain-case/endocast evidence on prefrontal expansion can serve as an indirect, correlated check, but not a direct date.

**Vocal-auditory communication**:

- Ontogeny: auditory system functional prenatally (cochlea mature ~20 weeks gestation, behavioral responsiveness by third trimester); cooing ~6–8 weeks; vocal play ~4–6 months; canonical babbling ~6–8 months, drifting toward native-language sound patterns by ~9–12 months. This is a genuine auditory-vocal loop, not motor output alone — shown by babbling being shaped by auditory input/feedback, and by deaf infants showing delayed/reduced vocal babbling while showing manual babbling when exposed to sign.
- Phylogeny: anatomically ancient and widespread across vertebrates — sound production/audition in fish and amphibians, long predating the amniote split.

**Conclusion from the two timelines**: meaningful vocal communication precedes object permanence, both ontogenetically and (on comparative, non-recapitulationist grounds) phylogenetically.

## 2. What that ordering implies about reference

- Saussurean reference = an arbitrary sign standing for a signified that need not be perceptually present. This requires holding an absent referent in mind — exactly what object permanence supplies.
- Therefore: a communication system operating before object permanence exists **cannot** be Saussurean-referential. There is no representational slot yet for "the thing this sound stands for, which isn't here."
- What such a system _can_ be: **indexical**. Signal tightly bound to a present, perceptible state (cf. vervet monkey alarm calls — "functionally referential," not symbolically referential, per Seyfarth, Cheney & Marler 1980). Meaning is fused with the present moment, not detachable from it.

## 3. Sound as constitutive of presence, not a pointer beyond it

- In an indexical, pre-permanence system, hearing/producing a sound is not "inferring an absent thing." Both sender and receiver need no permanence at all for the system to work — nothing in it requires representing an absence.
- The occurrence of the sound **is itself** taken as evidence of the object's/state's presence — not a symbol pointing elsewhere, but part of the same undifferentiated present. Producing a sound in the absence of the physical object is, for this mind, equivalent to the object's presence in whatever reality-state the utterance occupies.

## 4. What tense would this sound like, translated into modern grammar?

- Tense and mood are devices for marking a **gap** between utterance-time/reality and event-time/reality (past = before now, future = not yet, irrealis/subjunctive = not necessarily actual). All of them presuppose representing an event as distinct from the present perceptual state — exactly what a pre-permanence mind cannot do.
- So the predicted form is **not** simple present tense (which is itself one term in an opposition against past/future/hypothetical). It's a form that is **tense-and-mood neutral by necessity** — not through economy, but because the opposing categories don't exist to be elided from. Such a form should be usable, unchanged, wherever a modern language would need several different tenses/moods, because for the system producing it, "was / is / will be / may be" were never four options — only one undifferentiated condition.

## 5. लेट् as a structural correspondence

- लेट् (one of the ten लकार in Sanskrit grammar): archaic, rare, confined almost entirely to Vedic, described by the grammatical tradition as interchangeable with domains otherwise covered by other लकार (subjunctive-/injunctive-like uses among them), lacking the fixed tense/mood value assigned to the regular लकार system (Whitney 1889; Macdonell 1916).
- This is a feature-for-feature match to what §4 predicts a grammaticalized trace of tense-mood-undifferentiated utterance would look like.
- **Scope of the claim, stated carefully**: this is a _structural_ correspondence, not a _historical/derivational_ one. Not claiming लेट् literally descends from a pre-permanence signal system — that's a stronger claim than the evidence supports, and standard alternative explanations (eroded archaic category, grammarians' residual class for irregular forms, dialectal variation) remain live and unaddressed. What's being claimed: लेट्'s indeterminacy is exactly the signature a surviving trace of that stage would carry, if there were one. Left open for philological work: how and why.

## 6. Correcting the closure logic: experience vs. perception

- **Original (wrong) framing**: object permanence closes off the indexical mode once it emerges — "impossible to perceive/express once object permanence sets in."
- **Why that's wrong**: object permanence _adds_ a capacity; it doesn't delete the older one. Indexical processing is deep-rooted enough to persist alongside referential capacity for a long stretch after the latter becomes available — and to dominate over it whenever both are active for a given stimulus. (Note: modern one-way experience→sound expression — pain cries, gasps, laughter — is NOT valid evidence for this persistence, because it's a different, one-way channel that never completes into a two-way referential loop. It's orthogonal to this argument, not supporting evidence for it.)
- **The real distinction needed**: _experience_ vs. _perception_.
    - **Experience** = what limbic neurons register. A sound generates a state functionally equivalent to the object's actual presence — no separate representational tag marking the sound-triggered state as different from what the object's real presence would produce. This is the indexical mode.
    - **Perception** = what is carried by cortical processing. A sound triggers a symbolic/conceptual tag _about_ the object — representationally detached from the object's own presence. This is the referential mode.
    - As long as sound → experience remains possible, it will dominate/supersede sound → perception whenever both are active. The mere cortical _availability_ of perception does not retire limbic experience.

## 7. The corrected terminus post quem (TPQ) for लेट्

- **Not**: the point referential capacity became available (too early, too weak — doesn't close the loop).
- **Actual condition**: the point at which the **sound → experience** pathway itself becomes impossible — hearing a sound can no longer generate limbic, experiential activation of the absent object, only cortical, symbolic perception of it.
- Only after this closure does speech processing become predominantly cortex-centric rather than limbic-driven — and only then could a residual, tense-mood-undifferentiated form become identifiable as a distinct, marked, archaic category (i.e., as लेट्) standing apart from a now-dominant referential system.
- Reframing: लेट् is not a fossil of the stage that ended when object permanence began. It's a fossil of a stage that ended considerably later — when the limbic sound-to-experience channel itself finally closed.

## 8. Loose thread: hemisphere lateralization

- The proposed limbic → cortical shift in speech processing plausibly tracks onto the standard right-hemisphere/left-hemisphere lateralization story: right hemisphere more limbic-connected, more prosodic/affective; left hemisphere more cortical, more propositional/referential (in most humans).
- If the neurolinguistic lateralization literature offers an independent, testable proxy for the timing/degree of this shift, it could check the TPQ argument against something other than लेट्'s internal grammar alone. Not yet developed — flagged for later.
